**Subject:** The mistake that kills most sales
**Preview:** It happens right after you say the price.

Hey,

Most sales aren't lost in the pitch. They're lost in the 10 seconds after the price.

You say the price. There's silence. It feels awkward, so you start talking: you explain, you justify, you offer a discount nobody asked for.

That's over-selling. The moment you speak first, you tell the buyer you're not sure the price is fair.

The fix sounds simple and feels hard: **say the price once, calmly, then stay silent.** Let them speak first, even if it takes a while.

## Email 3 · day 2
**Subject:** "It's too expensive" (what to say instead of dropping your price)
**Preview:** It's almost never about the price.

Hey,

When someone says "it's too expensive", most people panic and drop the price.

Don't. Ask: **"Too expensive... compared to what?"** Then listen.

Most of the time it's not the price. It's doubt. They're not sure it will work for *them*.

So ask one more question: **"If price wasn't an issue, would this be the right choice for you?"**

If they say yes, you've found the real problem, and you solve that. Not the price.

## Email 4 · day 3
**Subject:** Why pros make 4 offers (and amateurs make 1)
**Preview:** Most people quit after the first no.

Hey,

Most people make one offer. If the answer is no, they give up.

Pros prepare an **offer loop**: a short ladder of options made in order, until the buyer accepts one. Full price, then a payment plan, then a longer plan. Many people buy on the second, third or fourth offer.

The rule that keeps it professional: **every time you give something, ask for something back.** More instalments? Then ask for a testimonial or two referrals. Otherwise it's market-stall haggling, and your status drops.
